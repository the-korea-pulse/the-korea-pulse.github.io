South Korea's benchmark Kospi rose 2.4% on Thursday, August 14, closing back above the 6,900 line and stretching its winning streak to five consecutive trading days — a run that has covered every session this week. The index briefly reclaimed the 7,000 level during morning trade before paring part of the advance, and the tech-heavy Kosdaq also finished higher for a fifth straight day despite slipping into negative territory at midday.

## A Morning Surge That Touched 7,000

The session opened with force. The Kospi jumped more than 2% at the start of trade and pushed through 7,000 intraday, a threshold that has served as a psychological marker for the market's recovery. The early strength was concentrated in semiconductor shares, which led the broader advance and at one point had lifted the main board's gain toward the top of its daily range.

The momentum did not hold in a straight line. By midday the Kospi's advance had cooled to around 1%, and the Kosdaq — which had risen alongside the main board in the morning — turned lower. The afternoon brought a second leg up: the Kospi firmed again into the close to lock in its 2.4% gain and a finish above 6,900, while the Kosdaq recovered enough to end the day in positive territory.

## Five Days of Gains, and What That Streak Means

Thursday's close completes a clean sweep for the week — five sessions, five advances, for both the Kospi and the Kosdaq. A streak of that length, capped by an intraday move through 7,000, marks the market's strongest stretch of the summer and shifts the question from whether the recovery has traction to whether it can consolidate above the levels it has just reclaimed.

The composition of the rally matters here. Semiconductor names did the heavy lifting on Thursday, which ties the index's fortunes closely to the chip cycle rather than to a broad-based re-rating of Korean equities. That concentration cuts both ways: it gives the rally a clear engine, but it also means the index's hold on 6,900 — and any durable move back through 7,000 — will depend heavily on whether chip-sector strength persists.

## The Line the Market Is Now Testing

The intraday failure to hold 7,000 leaves that level as the immediate ceiling, with 6,900 now serving as the floor the market defended into the close. The midday wobble, in which the Kosdaq briefly went negative while the Kospi's gain halved, is a reminder that the buying remains uneven beneath the surface. Still, a week without a single down session, a 2.4% daily gain, and a close at the highs of the recovery give the bulls the stronger hand heading into the next session.
