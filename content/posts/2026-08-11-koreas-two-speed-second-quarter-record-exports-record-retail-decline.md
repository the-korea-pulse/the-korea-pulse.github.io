South Korea's economy pulled in two opposite directions in the second quarter of 2026: exports climbed to $275.5 billion, the largest quarterly total since the government began compiling the statistic, while sales at large discount stores fell 9.4 percent from a year earlier — the steepest quarterly drop on record. The two figures, both released in the government's industrial activity report for June and the second quarter, capture an economy where a semiconductor-driven external boom is running far ahead of a strained domestic retail sector.

## Chips Carry Exports to an All-Time High

The export record — roughly 389 trillion won in local-currency terms — was built on a semiconductor upcycle led by Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, whose memory and chip businesses have been the main beneficiaries of booming global demand. The strength was not evenly spread: the quarter's gains came disproportionately from the largest exporters, leaving overall trade more concentrated among the top conglomerates than a year earlier.

That concentration cuts both ways. It means the headline record is real money flowing through Korea's largest manufacturers, but it also means the export engine is narrower than the aggregate number suggests. A downturn in the chip cycle would land on a small number of firms that now account for an outsized share of the total.

## Hypermarkets Post Their Worst Quarter on Record

At the other end of the economy, product sales at hypermarkets — the big-box discount chains that anchor much of Korean grocery and household retail — contracted 9.4 percent year on year, the largest decline since the data series began. The government's assessment links the drop to the turmoil surrounding Homeplus, one of the country's major hypermarket operators, which has been going through court-supervised corporate rehabilitation. Store-level disruption at a chain of that scale is large enough to move the national statistic on its own.

The Homeplus effect makes the record decline partly a one-company story rather than a pure read on consumer demand. But hypermarkets were already losing ground to e-commerce before the rehabilitation filing, and a near-double-digit quarterly contraction points to pressure on the format that predates any single corporate failure.

## An Economy Growing from the Outside In

Taken together, the two records describe a second quarter in which Korea's growth came from the outside in. Foreign demand for chips delivered an all-time high in shipments abroad, while a bellwether of domestic consumption posted its worst quarter ever. The gap raises the questions that will define the second half of the year: how long the semiconductor upcycle can sustain the export run, and whether household spending — and the retailers that depend on it — can close the distance before it turns.
