Workers at smaller Korean companies are less likely than those at large firms to remain with the same employer a year after returning from parental leave. The reported gap shows that access to leave is only part of the challenge: keeping a job after coming back matters, too.

## The gap appears after the return

The measure concerns employees who have finished leave and resumed work, then stayed with the same company. It does not show why others left. Childcare demands, working conditions and decisions to change jobs could all play a part, but the retention gap alone cannot establish their relative weight.

## Support for smaller employers

A lengthy absence can be harder to cover in a small workplace. South Korea’s labor ministry has identified replacement hiring and support for colleagues who share the workload as measures to help smaller employers manage parental leave. The reported retention gap makes the period after an employee returns a test of whether that support is working.
