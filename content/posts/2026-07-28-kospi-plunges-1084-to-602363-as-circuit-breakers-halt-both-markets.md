South Korea's benchmark KOSPI index collapsed 10.84% on Tuesday to finish at 6,023.63, one of the steepest single-day falls in the market's history and severe enough to trigger emergency trading halts on both of the country's equity boards. The rout, quickly dubbed a "Black Tuesday," forced the operator to pause trading first on the main index and then on the tech-heavy KOSDAQ as losses cascaded across the session.

## A Drop Deep Enough to Trip the Safeguards

The scale of Tuesday's decline is best measured by the machinery it set off. Korea Exchange applies automatic circuit breakers designed to force a cooling-off period when prices move violently, and the first tier engages once a benchmark falls roughly 8% from the prior close and holds there. A 10.84% slide on the KOSPI cleared that threshold with room to spare, halting trading on the main board before the selling spread to the KOSDAQ, which tripped its own breaker in turn.

Having both markets suspended on the same day underscores that the pressure was not confined to a single sector or a handful of large-cap names. When a circuit breaker fires, all trading pauses for a set interval before resuming, a mechanism intended to interrupt panic-driven order flow rather than to reverse the move itself.

## Where the Index Now Sits

At 6,023.63, the KOSPI surrendered in a single session a large block of the ground it had built up during its earlier climb above the 6,000 line. A decline of that magnitude in one day compresses months of incremental gains and resets the reference point for every position carried into Wednesday's open.

## The Questions Left Open at the Close

The immediate mechanics — how far the index fell and that safeguards were activated on both boards — are settled by the closing print. What remains unresolved is what drove the selling to such an extreme and whether the halts succeeded in draining the momentum or merely deferred it. The behavior of the market at the next open, the response from financial authorities, and any read on foreign versus domestic order flow will determine whether Tuesday marks a one-day shock or the start of a broader repricing.
