Samsung Biologics and its in-house union are racing to close a drawn-out wage and collective-bargaining standoff before the Chuseok holiday, after a mediation commissioner set September 20–22 as the effective cutoff for talks. All 44 disputed clauses in the collective agreement will be put back on the table for a full re-examination.

## First Mediation Yields No Deal but a Tighter Calendar

The two sides held their first post-mediation session on September 8 at the Incheon Regional Labor Relations Commission. No agreement was reached, but the session did produce a concrete roadmap: additional face-to-face bargaining on September 10–11, conducted under the mediator's observation, followed by a formal second mediation round on September 15. The commissioner urged both parties to finalize terms no later than September 22, effectively giving them two weeks to bridge gaps that have persisted for months.

## 44 Clauses Back on the Table

Rather than narrowing the agenda, the parties agreed to reopen every one of the 44 collective-agreement clauses still in contention. The scope covers longstanding issues around wages and working conditions at Samsung Biologics' Songdo campus, the world's largest contract biologic-manufacturing complex. A full-scope review raises the stakes for each remaining session but also signals willingness on both sides to pursue a comprehensive settlement instead of piecemeal fixes.

## Pre-Holiday Pressure Mounts

The September 22 target is not arbitrary. Chuseok—one of South Korea's two major national holidays—falls shortly afterward, and an unresolved dispute heading into the break would complicate production planning at a facility that runs around the clock for global pharmaceutical clients. The government has separately been rolling out broader Chuseok consumer-stabilization measures, underscoring the holiday's economic significance and the political sensitivity of unresolved labor friction in the lead-up.

## Road Ahead for the Songdo Giant

If the September 10–11 working sessions narrow the gap on key clauses, the September 15 mediation could move toward a framework deal. Failure to do so would leave fewer than seven days before the commissioner's deadline lapses, at which point the dispute could escalate to further legal or industrial-action procedures under Korean labor law. For Samsung Biologics—whose order book depends on predictable manufacturing timelines—an extended standoff carries commercial as well as labor-relations risk.
