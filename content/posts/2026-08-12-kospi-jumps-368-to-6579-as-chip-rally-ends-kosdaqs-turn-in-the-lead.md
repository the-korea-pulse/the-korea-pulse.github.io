South Korea's benchmark KOSPI closed at 6,579 on Wednesday, August 12, gaining 3.68% in its strongest session of the week as semiconductor shares powered the advance. The rally capped three days in which market leadership swung sharply between Seoul's two boards: the small-cap KOSDAQ opened the week with a 6.97% surge steep enough to trigger a program-buying sidecar, only to stall near flat — up just 0.12% — on the very day large caps took off.

## Wednesday's Advance Was a Large-Cap Story

The KOSPI opened higher on Wednesday and kept widening its gains through the session, moving past the 2% mark in early trading before touching 3% intraday around the 6,500 line and finishing at 6,579. Semiconductor names did the heavy lifting, a notable reversal from Tuesday morning, when weakness in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix had briefly dragged the benchmark down around 1% before it recovered.

The KOSDAQ told the opposite story. The junior board slipped into negative territory while the KOSPI accelerated, and its eventual 0.12% close amounted to standing still while the main board posted its biggest one-day gain in the stretch.

## Monday Belonged to the Small Caps

The week's rotation began from the other direction. On Monday, August 10, the KOSPI managed only a modest rebound — its first gain in three sessions — while the KOSDAQ climbed as much as 6.78% intraday, enough to activate the buy sidecar, a circuit mechanism that pauses program orders after sharp futures moves. The junior index closed the day up 6.97%, an unusually violent single-day move for the board and a stark contrast with the benchmark's marginal advance.

Tuesday, August 11, was the hinge between the two regimes. Both indexes opened lower and spent the morning underwater, with chip heavyweights leading the KOSPI down while the KOSDAQ clawed back to slightly positive ground. By the close, both had reversed: the KOSPI added 0.7% and the KOSDAQ 0.3%, a second straight day of gains but with none of Monday's ferocity.

## What the Divergence Says About Positioning

The pattern across the three sessions points to money rotating rather than arriving all at once. Monday's KOSDAQ spike, concentrated enough to trip the sidecar, suggests speculative flows chasing smaller names while the large-cap board lacked a catalyst. Once semiconductor momentum returned on Wednesday, that leadership inverted almost completely — a 3.68% benchmark gain against a KOSDAQ that barely closed positive after trading in the red for much of the day.

The dependence cuts both ways. Tuesday's session showed how quickly softness in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix can pull the KOSPI down on its own, and Wednesday showed the same concentration working in reverse. With the benchmark now at 6,579 after a move of this size, the immediate question is whether chip-sector strength broadens into the rest of the market or remains narrow enough that a single day of weakness in the largest names unwinds much of the gain — the risk Tuesday's morning slide previewed in miniature.
