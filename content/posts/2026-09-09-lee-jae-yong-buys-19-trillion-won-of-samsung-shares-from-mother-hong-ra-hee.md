Samsung Electronics chairman Lee Jae-yong is buying roughly 1.9 trillion won worth of the company's shares from his mother, Hong Ra-hee, honorary director of the Leeum Museum of Art — a transaction disclosed on September 9 and formalized in a securities trading plan report filed under Lee's name with the Financial Supervisory Service's electronic disclosure system, DART. The company put the stake changing hands at 0.11 percent of Samsung Electronics.

## A Family Transfer, Not a Market Sale

The defining feature of the deal is who sits on each side of it. Rather than placing the shares with institutional buyers through a block trade — the route that would put a stake of this size into outside hands — Hong is selling directly to her son. The effect is to concentrate ownership further in the chairman himself: the shares leave one family member's account and enter another's, and the controlling family's combined position in Korea's largest company is preserved rather than diluted.

That distinction matters for how the market reads the transaction. A sale into the open market by a member of the founding family is typically parsed for what it signals about the family's need for cash or its commitment to the company. A purchase by the sitting chairman, financed at a scale of 1.9 trillion won, points the other way — it is among the clearest forms of insider conviction a controlling shareholder can display, and it tightens the link between Lee's personal wealth and the company he runs.

## The Procedural Trail at DART

The filing itself is worth noting. The document lodged with DART is a trading plan report for executives and major shareholders — the disclosure Korean rules require when a company insider intends to trade the firm's securities. By naming Lee as the submitter, the report places the chairman personally, not a holding vehicle or affiliate, as the acquiring party. For a transaction between the two most prominent members of the founding family, routing the deal through the standard insider-disclosure regime also gives outside shareholders formal notice of the change before it settles into the shareholder register.

## Ownership at the Top of a 1938-Vintage Group

The purchase lands within a corporate structure whose center of gravity has always been the founding family. The Samsung group traces back to a trading business Lee Byung-chul established in Taegu on March 1, 1938, and after the founder's death in 1987 the original organization split into five separate groups, with Shinsegae, CJ, Hansol, and JoongAng spinning off. The electronics company that remained at the core grew into the world's largest information technology firm and semiconductor maker by 2017 revenue, and the group is now led by chairman Lee Jae-yong from its Samsung Town headquarters in Seoul.

Against that history, an intra-family share transfer of this size reads as another step in the long consolidation of the third-generation chairman's position. Each percentage point of Samsung Electronics held directly by Lee is a percentage point that does not depend on affiliate cross-holdings or family coordination to count toward control.

## What the Deal Leaves Open

The disclosure establishes the parties, the approximate value, and the stake — it does not, on its own, settle how the purchase is being funded or what Hong intends to do with the proceeds, and those questions will shape how analysts ultimately score the transaction. What is already clear is narrower but concrete: about 1.9 trillion won of Samsung Electronics stock is moving from mother to son, the chairman's direct stake is growing, and the family's grip on the company founded by his grandfather is, if anything, firmer than before.
