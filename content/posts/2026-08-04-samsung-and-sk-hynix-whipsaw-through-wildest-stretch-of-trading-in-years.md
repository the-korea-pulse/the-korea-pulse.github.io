Korea's two flagship chipmakers have just delivered three of the most erratic sessions in their recent trading history. After Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix soared more than 20% on July 31 — an unprecedented single-day move for stocks of their size — both names crashed 7–8% the following Monday under heavy foreign and institutional selling, then spent Tuesday lurching from a sharp early rally into a 3% decline before clawing back to finish marginally higher. The whiplash leaves investors with a market that has repriced the two companies dramatically in both directions within a single week, and with the striking fact that even after the historic rally, both stocks reportedly trade at roughly half the price targets analysts have set for them.

## A Record Surge, Then a Two-Day Stress Test

The sequence began on the final trading day of July, when both stocks posted gains exceeding 20% — a move described in Seoul as without precedent for the pair. The euphoria did not survive the weekend. On Monday, August 3, Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix closed down in the 8% range, with foreign and institutional investors selling in tandem, according to reports from Seoul.

Tuesday's session compressed the whole drama into a single day. The two stocks opened sharply higher, surrendered the entire advance and turned negative by more than 3%, then recovered abruptly in the closing minutes to end with slim gains. That intraday arc — from strong open to steep loss to last-minute rescue — is the clearest evidence yet that the market has not settled on what the two companies are worth after July's repricing.

## The Gap Between Price and Target

Perhaps the most telling detail in the week's coverage is not the volatility itself but the valuation backdrop: even at post-surge levels, both stocks are reported to sit near half of the average price targets published by brokerages. Analysts, in other words, had already penciled in far more upside than the July 31 explosion delivered. That gap cuts two ways. Bulls read it as evidence that the rally was a catch-up move with room to run; the Monday sellers evidently concluded that a 20% one-day gain deserved to be banked regardless of what target prices imply.

During the same stretch, routine executive shareholding reports for Samsung Electronics were filed with the Financial Supervisory Service's DART disclosure system — a reminder that insider stake movements are being documented even as the stock swings.

## Why These Two Stocks Are the Korean Market

The stakes extend well beyond two tickers. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, together with Micron, form the trio that dominates global memory chip production, which makes their share prices a proxy for both the Korean market and the worldwide memory cycle. SK Hynix alone generated 66.19 trillion won in revenue and 19.80 trillion won in net income in fiscal 2024, employing 46,863 people from its base in Icheon. Samsung, whose electronics arm anchors a group founded in 1938 and now chaired by Lee Jae-yong, ranked fifth globally in brand value in 2024.

SK Hynix's own history is a reminder that Korean memory has survived worse turbulence than a volatile week: the company began in 1983 as Hyundai Electronics under Hyundai Group founder Chung Ju-yung, traded as Hynix from 2001, and joined SK Group in 2012.

## What the Tape Is Actually Saying

Three sessions do not make a trend, but they do make a diagnosis: conviction is high on both sides and neither has won. The record rally shows how much money wants exposure to Korean memory; the 8% reversal shows how quickly institutions will harvest gains at these levels; Tuesday's rescued close shows dip-buyers standing ready underneath. Until the gap between market prices and analyst targets narrows from one direction or the other, more sessions like Tuesday's — violent, indecisive, and resolved only at the bell — look like the base case.
