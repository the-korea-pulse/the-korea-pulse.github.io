Housing businesses became slightly more optimistic about the Seoul metropolitan market in September, encouraged by rising home prices and expectations surrounding the government’s housing-supply measures. The improvement appears concentrated rather than broad-based, with price gains in northeastern Seoul and southern Gyeonggi helping to lift sentiment.

## Local Price Gains Support Confidence

Market strength in Seoul’s Nowon and Jungnang districts was cited as one factor behind the improved outlook. Rising transaction prices in southern Gyeonggi also contributed, indicating that business confidence is responding to activity in selected residential markets rather than to a uniform recovery across the capital region.

For developers and other housing businesses, firmer prices can improve expectations for sales, project feasibility and demand. A modest rise in sentiment, however, does not by itself establish that construction activity or housing transactions will accelerate.

## Supply Policy Adds a Second Source of Optimism

Expectations tied to the government’s housing-supply plans also supported the brighter assessment. Supply measures can affect the industry’s outlook by shaping expectations for land availability, approvals and future project opportunities.

The practical effect will depend on implementation. Timelines, project locations and financing conditions will determine whether policy-driven optimism translates into new development.

## Improvement Remains Uneven

The latest assessment points to a cautiously improving metropolitan market, led by particular areas where home prices have risen. It should not be read as evidence of a nationwide housing rebound.

The durability of the shift will depend on whether price momentum spreads beyond the areas already showing strength and whether anticipated supply initiatives advance into executable projects.
