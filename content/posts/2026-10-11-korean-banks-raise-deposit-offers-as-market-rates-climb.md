South Korean banks are competing for term deposits as market interest rates rise, with reports pointing to higher advertised returns. The broader data calls for a distinction: a bank’s offer on a particular deposit can rise even when the average rate across new deposits holds steady.

## Offers rise, but the average has paused

The Bank of Korea reported that the average rate on new bank deposits was **3.21% in August 2026**, unchanged from July. It had been **3.08% in June**. Those figures show that deposit rates rose over the summer, but they do not establish the rate available on any particular term deposit today. [Bank of Korea, August interest rates](https://www.bok.or.kr/eng/bbs/E0000634/view.do?menuNo=400069&nttId=11065099), [June interest rates](https://www.bok.or.kr/eng/bbs/E0000634/view.do?menuNo=400069&nttId=11063199)

## Why the competition matters

Higher market rates can affect what banks pay to attract deposits. For savers, the useful comparison is the rate they can actually obtain for the same term and deposit amount, including any conditions attached to a promotional offer. The central bank’s average provides context; it is not a substitute for checking each bank’s current terms. [Bank of Korea, monetary policy transmission](https://www.bok.or.kr/eng/main/contents.do?menuNo=400016)
