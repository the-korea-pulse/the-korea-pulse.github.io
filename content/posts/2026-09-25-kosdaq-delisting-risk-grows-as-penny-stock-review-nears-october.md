More South Korean stocks could face delisting decisions in October as the exchange applies tougher market value rules and a new test for shares priced below 1,000 won. A company placed on a watchlist still has a chance to recover; the designation does not itself end its listing. [Financial Services Commission](https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010104/86875).

## A higher bar for staying listed

Since July, KOSDAQ companies have needed a market value of at least 20 billion won, while the KOSPI threshold is 30 billion won. The July changes also introduced a delisting test for shares trading below 1,000 won on either market. [Financial Services Commission](https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010104/86875).

The Korea Exchange’s [watchlist records](https://kind.krx.co.kr/investwarn/adminissue.do?method=searchAdminIssueSub) show companies designated for falling short of market value requirements. The new share price rule gives low-priced stocks a separate route into the same review process.

## Why October matters

A stock enters the watchlist after spending 30 consecutive trading days below the applicable threshold. It then faces a 90-trading-day review period. To avoid delisting on that ground, it must clear the threshold for 45 consecutive trading days during that period. Those deadlines make October a potential decision point for companies designated after the July rules took effect. [Financial Services Commission](https://www.fsc.go.kr/po010104/86875).

The government has since said that companies meeting certain financial conditions may be allowed to transfer to the smaller KONEX market without a closing auction. It has also deferred the next planned increase in market value thresholds from January to July 2027. Neither change removes the thresholds now in force or makes delisting inevitable for a company currently on the watchlist. [Financial Services Commission](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/87648).
