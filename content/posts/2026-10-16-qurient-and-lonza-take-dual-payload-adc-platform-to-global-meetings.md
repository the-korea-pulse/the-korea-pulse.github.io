Qurient and Lonza are promoting their jointly developed dual-payload antibody-drug conjugate platform, SYN-duo CC, at international industry meetings. The presentations put the technology before potential partners, but they do not establish that it has secured customers or demonstrated clinical benefits.

## From San Diego to London

Lonza introduced SYN-duo CC at World ADC in San Diego, and the platform was subsequently presented at an ELRIG drug discovery conference in London. The appearances extend its exposure beyond a single event as the companies pursue global commercialization.

A dual-payload ADC is designed to carry two drug components on an antibody. That design may give developers more options in constructing a treatment, but its value will depend on evidence from specific drug candidates. The conference presentations alone do not show how the platform performs in patients.

## The commercial question

For Qurient, the next measure of progress is whether interest in the platform turns into development work or commercial agreements. The company’s regulatory filing provides financial context: revenue changed **+3.42% (Q2 2025 → Q2 2026)**, while its operating margin was **−672.86% (Q2 2026)**. Those figures describe the filed periods; they do not measure the outcome of the Lonza collaboration.
