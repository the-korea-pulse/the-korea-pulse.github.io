Large Korean companies are settling their bills with subcontractors before the Chuseok holiday rather than after it, a move that puts cash into smaller firms during the most expensive week of the Korean calendar. K Shipbuilding has brought forward roughly 25.3 billion won in payments to its partner companies, and Hanwha Group chairman Kim Seung-youn has directed the group to clear supplier invoices ahead of the holiday, framing partner-company workers as part of the Hanwha "family."

## Why the Timing Matters for Small Suppliers

Chuseok is the point in the year when Korean employers face the heaviest concentration of outflows: holiday bonuses, gift allowances, and in many small firms a full pay cycle landing in the same week. Subcontractors in heavy industry typically wait weeks for invoices to clear, so a payment that arrives before the holiday instead of after it changes whether a supplier borrows to make payroll or does not.

That is why early settlement has become a standard gesture from large conglomerates in the run-up to the holiday. It costs the paying company only the timing of cash it already owes, while the receiving firms avoid short-term financing at the moment they need liquidity most.

## K Shipbuilding's 25.3 Billion Won

K Shipbuilding, the mid-sized yard formerly known as STX Offshore & Shipbuilding, has moved about 25.3 billion won of payments forward for its partner firms. For a shipbuilder of its scale, the figure represents a meaningful slice of what it owes the fabrication, outfitting, and painting contractors that make up most of a modern yard's workforce. Shipbuilding is unusually dependent on in-house subcontractors, so the ripple effect of a single yard's payment decision reaches a large number of small employers in the surrounding region.

## Hanwha's Group-Wide Instruction

At Hanwha, the direction came from the top. Chairman Kim Seung-youn called partner-company employees part of the group's own household and ordered payments to suppliers to be completed before the holiday. Hanwha spans defense, shipbuilding through Hanwha Ocean, chemicals, energy, and finance, so a group-level instruction touches a broad and varied supplier base rather than a single industry.

The scale of Hanwha's accelerated payments was not disclosed in the reports available, and this article does not estimate it.

## The Government Backdrop

The corporate moves land alongside the Ministry of Economy and Finance's Chuseok livelihood stabilization package, the government's annual set of measures aimed at keeping household and small-business cash flow steady through the holiday. The corporate early-payment announcements and the government package pull in the same direction: both are designed to prevent a liquidity squeeze at the smallest firms during the holiday week.

## What Early Payment Does and Does Not Change

Early settlement is a short-term liquidity measure, not a change in contract terms. The suppliers receive money they were already owed, only sooner. It does not address the underlying payment periods, unit prices, or the bargaining position of subcontractors relative to the large firms they serve, which remain the longer-running policy questions in Korea's supplier relations. For the holiday itself, however, the effect is immediate and concrete for the firms on the receiving end.
