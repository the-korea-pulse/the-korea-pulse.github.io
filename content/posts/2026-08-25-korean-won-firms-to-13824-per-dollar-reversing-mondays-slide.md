The Korean won strengthened against the U.S. dollar on Tuesday, with the 15:30 reference rate set at 1,382.4 won per dollar, 4.1 won below the prior session. The move more than unwound Monday's weakening, when the rate had risen 3.7 won to 1,386.1, and left the currency pair roughly where it began the week.

## A Two-Day Round Trip

Monday's session lifted the dollar to 1,386.1 won at the daily reference fixing, extending the won's stay in the upper half of the 1,380s. Tuesday took nearly all of that back. Over the two sessions, dollar-won traded inside a band only about four won wide, between the low 1,380s and the mid 1,380s — a range of well under half a percent from top to bottom.

Neither daily move was large by the pair's own standards. Tuesday's 4.1-won decline works out to roughly 0.3 percent, and Monday's rise was smaller still. Swings of that size describe routine two-way trade rather than a directional break: dollar demand firm enough to keep the rate above 1,380, but no follow-through to push it decisively higher.

## Why the 15:30 Print Is the Number That Counts

The 15:30 rate is the Seoul market's daily benchmark — the weighted-average price that banks, corporates and official statistics use as the day's representative dollar-won level. Since Korea extended onshore trading hours into the night in mid-2024, it no longer marks the actual end of dealing, but it remains the reference against which each day's change is measured, which is why both sessions' moves are quoted from it.

## What the 1,380s Say About Positioning

The takeaway from the two fixings is stasis at a weak-won level. A rate holding in the 1,380s keeps import costs and dollar-denominated liabilities under pressure for Korean firms, while cushioning exporters' won-converted revenues. At the same time, the speed with which Tuesday erased Monday's move suggests the market is trading the range rather than testing its edges: dips toward 1,382 have been bought, and pushes toward 1,386 have been sold, at least across these two sessions.

Until the pair closes clearly outside that band, the daily changes are better read as noise around an equilibrium the market has settled on for now — one in which the won is weak, but not getting weaker.
