Samsung Electronics and SK hynix ended the trading week on a strong note, with Samsung closing up 3.4% and SK hynix jumping 6.4% on Friday, September 18. The rally erased a modest Thursday pullback and extended a three-session stretch in which Korea's two largest chipmakers moved in the same direction on every day but with sharply different amplitudes.

## Three Sessions, Three Different Moods

The week's action for the two stocks unfolded in distinct phases.

- **Wednesday, September 16:** Both stocks finished higher, with Samsung gaining roughly 2% and SK hynix about 4%. The day was defined by a split between investor groups: foreign investors were net sellers while domestic institutions were net buyers, and institutional demand was enough to lift both names.
- **Thursday, September 17:** The pair drifted through the session and closed slightly lower. Samsung slipped 0.4% and SK hynix fell 0.8%, giving back a small portion of Wednesday's advance.
- **Friday, September 18:** Buying resumed from the opening bell. Samsung was up around 2.6% and SK hynix around 4.2% in early trading, and the gains widened through the session to a 3.4% and 6.4% close respectively.

## Why SK hynix Keeps Moving Twice as Far

Across all three sessions, SK hynix's daily move was roughly double Samsung's in percentage terms, both on the way up and on the way down. That pattern is consistent with the two companies' business mix. SK hynix is a pure-play memory maker, so its share price is a more concentrated bet on memory pricing and demand. Samsung's memory division sits alongside foundry, mobile, display and consumer electronics businesses, which dilutes the stock's sensitivity to any single memory-cycle headline.

The Friday session also stands out because the early-session gains did not fade. Intraday spikes in Korean large caps often narrow into the close as short-term traders take profits. On September 18 the opposite happened: both stocks closed well above their morning levels, which points to sustained buying into the afternoon rather than a brief opening squeeze.

## Investor Flows Behind the Swings

The clearest signal about who was driving prices came on Wednesday, when foreign investors sold and institutions bought. Foreign holders own a large share of both companies, so their net selling typically weighs on prices. That Wednesday still closed higher shows domestic institutional demand was strong enough to absorb the foreign supply. The subsequent Thursday dip and Friday rebound fit a market where competing flows, rather than a single decisive catalyst, set the day-to-day direction.

## A Shareholder Filing at Samsung

Separately, Samsung Electronics submitted a report to the Financial Supervisory Service's disclosure system covering a change in shareholdings by its largest shareholder and related parties. The filing itself is a routine regulatory notice that companies must submit when holdings of the controlling shareholder group change. The disclosure available at the time of writing does not specify the size or direction of the change, so no conclusion can be drawn about whether the filing is related to the week's share-price moves.

## What the Week Tells Investors

Taken together, the three sessions show a market willing to bid aggressively for Korean memory exposure on strong days while stepping back quickly on weak ones. SK hynix remains the higher-beta way to express that view, and Friday's close leaves both stocks with a net gain for the three-day period despite the Thursday interruption. Whether the Friday momentum carries into the following week will depend largely on whether foreign investors return as net buyers or continue to sell into institutional demand.
