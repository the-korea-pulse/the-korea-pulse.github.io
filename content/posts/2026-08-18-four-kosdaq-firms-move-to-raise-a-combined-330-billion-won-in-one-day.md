Four KOSDAQ-listed companies disclosed decisions on August 18 to raise fresh capital through new share issuance, with the combined total reaching roughly 330 billion won. Silicon2 (257720) accounted for the bulk of it, approving a third-party allotment worth about 300 billion won, while Kespion (079190), SSR (275630), and iA (038880) announced smaller raises of about 13 billion won, 7 billion won, and 10 billion won respectively. All four companies cited operating funds among the stated purposes.

## One Rights Offering Among Three Private Placements

The structure of the four deals splits along a familiar line in the Korean market. Silicon2, Kespion, and SSR each chose a third-party allotment, in which new shares go to designated investors rather than the open market. SSR's 7 billion won issue names Jiran Security & Communication among the allottees, giving that deal the character of a strategic placement rather than a purely financial one.

iA took the other route: a roughly 10 billion won rights offering allocated to existing shareholders. That choice spreads both the funding burden and the dilution across the current shareholder base instead of concentrating new ownership in outside hands. Consistent with that structure, iA has filed with the Financial Supervisory Service's electronic disclosure system (DART) to set a shareholder record date, the procedural step that fixes which holders are entitled to subscribe. SSR filed a record-date notice of its own on the same system.

## Why the Silicon2 Raise Stands Apart

At about 300 billion won, Silicon2's placement is more than twenty times the size of the next-largest deal in the group and represents a substantial injection for a KOSDAQ-listed company. A raise of that scale through a third-party allotment typically points to a sizable committed investor and an expansion agenda beyond routine working-capital needs, though the company's disclosure frames the purpose broadly as operating funds and related uses. The identity and terms attached to the allottees will determine how much of the raise translates into strategic partnership rather than simple balance-sheet reinforcement.

## What Dilution and Discounts Will Decide

For shareholders in all four companies, the immediate questions are mechanical: the issue price relative to the market price, the resulting dilution, and any lock-up conditions binding the new investors. Third-party allotments in Korea are subject to pricing rules that limit discounts, but the arrival of a large new holder can still shift a company's ownership structure meaningfully — a consideration that looms largest at Silicon2 given the size of its raise. For iA's rights offering, participation rates among existing shareholders will show how much conviction the current base has in the company's funding plan, with the record date now being set through the DART filing.

The cluster of same-day announcements also says something about conditions on the KOSDAQ: companies are finding windows to issue equity, and at least some outside investors are willing to take concentrated positions in smaller listed firms. Whether that willingness persists will depend on how these newly issued shares perform once they hit the register.
