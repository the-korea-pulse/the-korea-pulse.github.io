South Korea’s autumn recruitment season is gathering pace, with CJ and Korea Investment & Securities launching separate entry-level hiring campaigns. CJ says its intake will be its largest in four years, while the brokerage will accept applications until Oct. 19 and plans a recruitment briefing involving senior executives.

## CJ Expands Its Entry-Level Intake

CJ’s decision to increase hiring marks a notable shift at a time when many young job seekers face a cautious corporate labor market. The group has not disclosed a precise hiring target in the announcement, but its description of the campaign as the largest in four years indicates a broader intake than in its recent recruitment cycles.

The expansion could carry particular weight because large conglomerates recruit across multiple business lines, potentially giving graduates access to a wider range of corporate roles. Details such as openings, eligibility requirements and application schedules will remain important for assessing where the additional hiring is concentrated.

## Brokerage Adds Executive Access to Recruitment

Korea Investment & Securities is running its second-half graduate recruitment campaign through Oct. 19. Alongside the application process, the company plans a recruitment presentation attended by members of management.

Senior-level participation may give applicants a clearer view of the brokerage’s business priorities and the qualities it expects from new employees. It also gives the company an opportunity to compete more directly for candidates interested in finance, where career development and access to decision-makers can influence employer choice.

Regulatory filings identify Korea Investment & Securities as the submitting entity for both a shelf-registration investment prospectus and a securities issuance performance report, underscoring the capital-markets operations behind its recruitment effort.

## A Broader Contest for Graduate Talent

The two campaigns reflect different hiring messages. CJ is emphasizing the scale of its intake, while Korea Investment & Securities is pairing recruitment with direct engagement from executives.

For applicants, the announcements broaden the range of large-employer opportunities available during the second-half hiring period. The larger question is whether these campaigns remain isolated corporate initiatives or develop into a wider increase in graduate recruitment across Korea’s major business groups and financial companies.
