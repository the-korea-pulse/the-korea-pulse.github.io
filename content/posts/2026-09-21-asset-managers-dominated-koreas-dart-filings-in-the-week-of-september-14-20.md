Korea's corporate disclosure traffic in the week of September 14 to 20, 2026 was driven less by listed companies than by asset managers moving money through registered vehicles. A count of 100 filings received by the Financial Supervisory Service's Data Analysis, Retrieval and Transfer system (DART) during the week spans 64 distinct filing types, but the top of the table is dominated by a single category: registration applications for asset transfers, with 11 filings. Fund managers account for the largest filers by a wide margin.

## Asset-transfer registrations led the week

The most common filing type was the registration application for asset transfers, at 11. Reports on securities ownership by executives and major shareholders followed with 9. Audit reports came next at 4, while registration applications for asset securitization plans and shelf registrations for trust-type collective investment securities recorded 3 each.

Together these five categories make up 30 of the 100 filings. The remaining 70 are spread across 59 other filing types, which means the long tail of routine paperwork is broad but thin: most categories appeared only once or twice during the week.

The pairing of asset-transfer registrations with asset-securitization plan filings points to structured-finance activity as the week's main engine. Both filing types are steps in packaging assets into securities, and the concentration in the same week is consistent with vehicle setup work by financial institutions rather than operating-company events.

## Hanwha Asset Management filed nearly one in five

By filer, Hanwha Asset Management submitted 19 filings, the most of any entity and close to a fifth of the week's total. Kyobo AXA Asset Management followed with 9. MGENSolution filed 4, and IBK Asset Management and Juyeon Tech each filed 3.

The three asset-management firms among the top five filers together produced 31 filings, or just under a third of everything received. The top five filers combined account for 38 of the 100. The skew is notable: the week's disclosure volume is less a picture of broad market activity than of a handful of fund houses processing product registrations.

## Audit reports still surface for June fiscal closes

Among the four audit reports, one came from Navis Automotive Systems, covering the fiscal period ending June 2026. The report was submitted by Dahyun Accounting Corporation. Audit reports for June year-ends arriving in mid-September reflect the roughly three-month gap between fiscal close and the filing of an audited statement.

## Reading the shape of the week

The distribution suggests two things about Korean disclosure flow in this period. First, structured-product and fund paperwork can swamp the headline count without signalling anything about listed-company fundamentals. A reader scanning DART for corporate news would find only a minority of the week's filings relevant. Second, insider and major-shareholder ownership reports remain a steady second-tier category at 9 filings, which keeps them the most useful line for tracking who is buying and selling stakes in listed firms.

The figures cover filings received during 2026-W38 as tallied from DART, the electronic disclosure system operated by the Financial Supervisory Service.
