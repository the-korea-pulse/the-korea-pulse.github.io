More than half of the drop of over 190,000 in the number of employed young Koreans recorded last month came from industries classified as highly exposed to artificial intelligence — chiefly information and communications, along with professional, scientific and technical services. That concentration marks a change in where the labor-market pain is landing: a year earlier, youth job losses were centered on manufacturing and on accommodation and food services.

## The Decline Moves Upmarket

The sectors now shedding the most young workers are the ones that a generation of Korean graduates was told to aim for. Information and communications — the home of software developers, platform operators and telecom firms — and professional services such as consulting, research and engineering have long absorbed the country's most credentialed young job seekers. Domestic coverage of the figures captured the dissonance bluntly, invoking the familiar figure of the childhood "computer whiz" who was supposed to be set for life.

Last year's pattern was easier to file under a conventional downturn story: factory employment softening with exports, and hospitality jobs fluctuating with domestic demand. Those losses hit young workers too, but they fit a cyclical template. A contraction led by IT and knowledge-intensive services is a different shape of shock, because these were the sectors expected to keep hiring through the cycle.

## Why the AI Label Is Doing Real Work

Calling these "high AI-exposure" industries is a classification, not proof of cause. The monthly data show where jobs disappeared, not why. Tech and platform companies have also been tightening hiring for other reasons, from weaker funding conditions to post-expansion cost cutting, and a single month of data cannot separate those forces.

Still, the composition matters. The tasks that dominate entry-level work in these fields — junior coding, document drafting, basic research and analysis — sit squarely within what current AI tools automate most readily. If firms are meeting demand with fewer junior hires rather than cutting existing staff, the effect would surface exactly where it now appears: in the employment counts of the youngest cohort, in the most AI-exposed sectors, before it shows up anywhere else.

## A Narrower Doorway, Not Just a Downturn

That is what makes this reading of the numbers uncomfortable. Cyclical job losses reverse when the cycle turns. A structural thinning of entry-level white-collar work would instead narrow the doorway through which young Koreans convert education into careers — and the sectors now leading the decline are precisely the ones that doorway used to open onto. Whether the coming months confirm a temporary hiring pause or the start of that structural shift is now the question hanging over the youth employment data.
