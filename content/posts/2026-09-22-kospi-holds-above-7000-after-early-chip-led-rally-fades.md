The KOSPI ended September 22 marginally higher after an early rally of more than 2%, fueled by overnight gains in US semiconductor names, lost most of its momentum through the afternoon. The index kept its footing above the 7,000 mark, a level that has become the market's psychological floor since it was first crossed, but the session's shape tells a more cautious story than the opening print suggested.

## A Strong Open That Did Not Hold

The day began with a clear tailwind. Chip stocks in the United States had advanced in the prior session, and Korean semiconductor heavyweights followed at the open, lifting the benchmark past a 1% gain within the first hour and briefly above 2%. That is the pattern Seoul traders have come to expect: the KOSPI's exposure to memory and foundry names means it often trades as a leveraged proxy for global chip sentiment.

What changed by midday was the follow-through. Buying interest thinned as the session progressed, and the index drifted back toward the flat line. By the close it was up only fractionally, the sort of finish Korean market reports describe as "strong early, weak late." The 7,000 threshold held, but the intraday reversal erased most of the paper gains booked in the morning.

## Why Chips Set the Tone in Both Directions

Semiconductors account for an outsized share of KOSPI market capitalization, so a single sector's mood can swing the whole index. When US chipmakers rally, Korean peers tend to gap higher at the open. But that concentration cuts the other way: once the initial enthusiasm is priced in, there are few other sectors large enough to keep the index climbing on their own. The fade seen on September 22 is consistent with that structural dependence rather than with any fresh negative news.

The policy backdrop reinforces how central the sector has become. The Ministry of Economy and Finance issued a press release on a site visit to a semiconductor facility by its strategic economic advisory group, underscoring that chip competitiveness remains a standing item on the government's economic agenda. The release itself did not include details on the outcome of the visit, so its market significance lies mainly in signaling continued official attention rather than in any specific measure.

## Reading the Close

For investors, the relevant question is whether a narrow, chip-dependent advance can sustain the index at record territory. The September 22 session offers a mixed answer. On one hand, the KOSPI absorbed a sharp intraday pullback without breaching 7,000, which suggests dip-buying interest remains present. On the other, the inability to hold a 2% gain points to limited conviction beyond the semiconductor trade.

Whether the index builds on this level will depend on whether the US chip rally has further to run and whether other sectors, from autos to financials, begin to share the load. Until that broadening appears, sessions like this one, with a fast start and a flat finish, are likely to recur.
