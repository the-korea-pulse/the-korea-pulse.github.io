July 2026 apartment filings in the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport's real-transaction disclosure system point to one dominant pattern in the capital region: buyers are paying more per square meter for three-decade-old small flats in Seongnam's Bundang district than for nearly new, full-sized apartments inside Seoul. A 36-square-meter unit at Geumgok Cheongsol Jugong Complex 9, completed in 1995, changed hands for 1.045 billion won on July 11 — roughly 29 million won per square meter. That is more than the 26 million won per square meter fetched by an 84-square-meter unit at Godeok Arteon in Seoul's Gangdong district, a 4,066-household complex completed in 2020, which sold for 2.185 billion won on July 5.

## The Land Under the Apartment, Not the Apartment

Bundang's July trades read less like purchases of homes than of future building rights. At Jeongja Hansol Maeul Jugong Complex 5, a 12-building estate occupied since October 1994, a fourth-floor 41-square-meter unit sold for 1.27 billion won on July 16. The neighboring Jugong Complex 4 saw the same floor plan trade at 1.2 billion won on July 15 and 1.19 billion won six days later. At Geumgok Cheongsol Jugong 9, a 42-square-meter unit went for 1.25 billion won on July 11, days after an eighth-floor unit of the same type sold for 1.2 billion won.

These are prices that make little sense for the flats as they stand — cramped 1990s construction with per-square-meter values above new Seoul towers. They are consistent with expectations that Bundang, the most prized of Korea's first-generation new towns, will be rebuilt under the government's redevelopment framework, with today's small units converting into stakes in tomorrow's larger ones. Newer Bundang-area stock traded firmly too: a 115-square-meter unit at Sanun Maeul 13 in Unjung-dong sold for 1.885 billion won on July 10, and a first-floor 59-square-meter unit at Wirye Jayeon&Central Xi went for 1.73 billion won on July 9.

## Ilsan Tells the Other Half of the Story

The same disclosure data shows how unevenly that redevelopment premium is distributed. Ilsan in Goyang — a first-generation new town of the same vintage as Bundang — recorded a 59-square-meter unit at Pungdong Supsok Maeul 8 selling for just 325 million won on July 17, and a similar-sized unit at Janghang Hosu Maeul 2 Hyundai for 440 million won. That is roughly five to six million won per square meter, about a fifth of Bundang's level for comparable-age housing. Even Ilsan's newest large complex, the 2,404-household Y-City in Baekseok-dong completed in 2016, topped out at 1.14 billion won for a 103-square-meter unit.

The gap extends beyond the capital region. In Busan's Hwamyeong-dong, a 115-square-meter unit at Lotte Castle Kaiser — a 5,239-household complex — sold for 710 million won on July 30. A Bundang buyer paid nearly twice that in July for a unit one-third the size.

## Seoul and Gwanggyo Hold the Upper Band

Within Seoul, Gangdong district produced a dense cluster of trades: besides the Godeok Arteon sale, a 59-square-meter unit at Godeok Central Purgio sold for 1.65 billion won on July 7, a 58-square-meter unit at Amsa Seonsa Hyundai for 1.52 billion won on July 14, and a 59-square-meter unit at Gangil River Park 3 for 1.25 billion won. In Gangnam, a 34-square-meter unit at Suseo Kkachi Maeul — another 1993-vintage complex — fetched 1.545 billion won on July 28, echoing the Bundang pattern of old, small and expensive.

Notably, Gwanggyo in Suwon now trades shoulder to shoulder with eastern Seoul: an 84-square-meter unit at Gwanggyo Jayeon&Hillstate sold for 2.1 billion won on July 11, within striking distance of Godeok Arteon's price for the same floor plan. In Yongin's Suji district, a 99-square-meter unit at Seongbok Station Lotte Castle Gold Town went for 1.945 billion won.

## Wide Spreads Inside Single Complexes

The July data also shows how much floor level and timing moved prices within identical floor plans. At Yeongtong Byeokjeokgol Lotte in Suwon, a 20th-floor 49-square-meter unit sold for 550 million won on July 9, while a first-floor unit of the same type went for 370 million won four days later — a 49 percent premium for elevation. In Songdo, Incheon, a 35th-floor 59-square-meter unit at The Sharp Central City brought 747 million won on July 11; an identical sixth-floor unit sold for 95 million won less on July 29. At Yeongtong Hwanggol Jugong 1 in Suwon, the same 59-square-meter type traded at 580 million won on July 15 and 645 million won on July 30 — a 65 million won jump inside two weeks.

For buyers reading the July record, the message is uncomfortable but clear: in the capital region's redevelopment-candidate districts, the market is pricing land and entitlement, not livable space — and the spread between the towns that expect rebuilding and those still waiting has rarely looked wider.
